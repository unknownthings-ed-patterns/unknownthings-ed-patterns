# Educational Pattern Language Wiki

Welcome! This site is written in Japanese, but you can read it in your language using the **Translate** button at the bottom of any page (powered by Google Translate).

---

## What is this site?

This is a personal knowledge wiki on **educational practice**, built and maintained by **Hiroshi Iwai** (岩井輝久), an elementary school teacher in Japan specializing in special education (特別支援学級 — resource rooms for students with social/emotional and developmental needs).

The wiki is organized around **Pattern Language** — a framework originally developed by architect Christopher Alexander and adapted here for teaching. Each "pattern" describes a recurring problem in educational settings and a proven solution, with context, forces, and connections to related patterns.

---

## What will you find here?

### Math Teaching
- **Number Talks** — a 5-minute warm-up routine where students solve one mental math problem and share multiple strategies
- **Math Workshop** — a structured class format combining mini-lessons, independent work, math journals, and reflection
- **Building Thinking Classrooms** — a task-based approach (Liljedahl & Giroux, 2024) with 136 tasks designed for low floor / high ceiling engagement
- **Thinking Tasks Collection** — 136 math tasks covering K–6 curriculum, each with scaffolding at three levels (Mild / Medium / Spicy) and consolidation questions

### Special Education
- Instructional strategies for mixed-grade resource rooms
- Adapting mainstream math approaches for students with diverse needs
- Anchor tasks and portfolio systems

### Pattern Language for Education
- Over 100 patterns covering classroom environment, learning structures, vocabulary instruction, motivation, and more
- Connections between patterns form a navigable network

## About the author

Hiroshi Iwai is an elementary school teacher and researcher interested in the intersection of **Pattern Language**, **writing workshop** (Nancy Atwell), and **mathematics education**. His research connects Tsunesaburo Makiguchi's 文型応用主義 (type-based learning) with modern approaches like Building Thinking Classrooms.

He is also the author of *Tomigaki Education and Analogical Learning* (とみ書き教育における類推的学習), exploring connections between Makiguchi, Atwell, and analogical reasoning in arithmetic.
